# How much to charge for a YouTube sponsorship

Charge your average views per video, divided by 1,000, times a CPM (price per 1,000 views) that fits your niche and size. In 9,002 sold integrations tracked by ThoughtLeaders, channels under 100K subscribers got a median of $600 for a 60-90 second integration, and channels with 100K-500K subscribers got a median of $1,200.

That's the short version. The rest of this guide shows where those numbers come from, how to turn them into a quote for your channel, and what to add on top.

## What brands actually paid in 2026

The best data we've found comes from sold deals, not surveys. [ThoughtLeaders published prices for 9,002 YouTube integrations](https://thoughtleaders.ai/blog/2026-10-15-youtube-sponsorship-cost-2026/) booked between August 2024 and August 2026:

| Channel size | Typical price (middle half of deals) | Median | Median cost per 1,000 projected views |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 100K subs | $370 - $1,000 | $600 | $36 |
| 100K - 500K subs | $750 - $2,000 | $1,200 | $26 |
| 500K - 1M subs | $1,050 - $3,000 | $2,000 | $21 |
| 1M - 5M subs | $2,000 - $5,250 | $3,500 | $19 |
| 5M+ subs | $2,475 - $10,500 | about $6,000 | $17 |

Two things jump out. First, price grows a lot slower than subscriber count. Second, small channels charge more per view than big ones. If you're under 100K subs, a CPM in the $30s is normal in this data, not greedy.

## The formula: views x CPM

Almost every sponsor prices off views, not subscribers. The math is:

**(Average views per video / 1,000) x CPM = your base rate**

A real example from our data: one creator on r/PartneredYoutube [described pricing a 60-second end-of-video read](https://www.reddit.com/r/PartneredYoutube/comments/zsrm73/how_much_do_you_get_paid_for_sponsorships/) as a $20 CPM times the average views of their last ~10 videos. With about 200,000 average views, that's a $4,000 flat fee.

[1of10's 2026 guide](https://www.1of10.com/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rates/) uses the same approach and puts typical sponsorship CPMs at $10 to $50 per 1,000 expected views. Their example: a channel averaging 25,000 views per video can reasonably quote $500 to $1,250 for a 60-second integration.

A few rules for picking the views number:

- Use the median of your last 10 videos, not your best video.
- Use views at a fixed age (30 days is common), not lifetime views.
- Leave out obvious outliers in both directions.

## Your niche moves the CPM

Brands pay more for viewers who are likely to buy something expensive. [TrendReport's modeled estimates](https://trendreport.com/intelligence/youtube-sponsorship-rates-by-niche/), built from tens of thousands of sponsored videos, give these per-1,000-view ranges:

- Business and finance: $18 - $47 (the highest per-view rate in their data)
- Education: $16 - $42
- Tech: $15 - $41
- Travel: $15 - $41
- Beauty, food, fitness, lifestyle: $15 - $40
- Gaming: $14 - $38

So a finance channel and a gaming channel with the same views can quote quite different numbers. Check your niche page, like [finance](/sponsorrateradar/rates/finance.html), [tech](/sponsorrateradar/rates/tech.html) or [gaming](/sponsorrateradar/rates/gaming.html), to see every sourced row for it.

## Price each format differently

"A sponsorship" can mean very different amounts of work. 1of10 gives these multipliers against a standard 60-90 second integration:

- Dedicated video: 2x to 4x
- 30-second mention: 0.5x to 0.7x
- Sponsored Short: 0.25x to 0.5x
- Community post: 0.1x to 0.2x
- Description link plus pinned comment, as an add-on: +5 to +10 percent

[Aspire's survey of 1,200 creators](https://www.aspire.io/blog/how-much-do-youtube-influencers-charge-for-sponsored-videos) points the same way from the other direction: integrations come in at roughly 50-60% of a dedicated video's rate, and even nano creators had a median of $700 for a dedicated video.

## Add-ons you should charge for

The base rate covers one placement in one video. Anything beyond that is a separate line:

- **Usage rights.** If the brand wants to run your clip as a paid ad, 1of10 puts that at +30 to +100 percent of the base fee.
- **Exclusivity.** Not working with competitors for 30 to 90 days costs you future deals; 1of10 lists +20 to +50 percent as typical.
- **Extra deliverables.** Pinned comments, community posts and extra links are add-ons, not freebies.

## A worked example

Say you run a tech channel and your last 10 videos have a median of 30,000 views at day 30.

1. Pick a CPM. Tech runs $15-$41 in TrendReport's data, and channels under 100K subs had a median of $36 in ThoughtLeaders' data. Something in the high $20s to mid $30s is defensible.
2. Do the math: 30 x $30 = $900 for a 60-90 second integration.
3. Sanity check: $900 sits inside the $370-$1,000 middle band for sub-100K channels. Good.
4. Build your other prices off it: a dedicated video at 2x-4x is $1,800-$3,600; a 30-second mention at 0.5x-0.7x is $450-$630.
5. Add usage rights or exclusivity only if the brand asks for them, and price them on top.

## Common mistakes

- **Pricing off subscribers.** A channel with fewer subs but more views per video is worth more to a sponsor.
- **Using your viral video's views.** Brands will check. Quote what you reliably get.
- **Giving away usage rights.** If the contract says the brand can use your video "in perpetuity, in all media," that's worth real money.
- **Never raising your rate.** If a sponsor rebooks you, that's proof the deal worked for them. ThoughtLeaders found 35% of brands booked the same channel again within a year.

## FAQ

### What is a good CPM for a YouTube sponsorship?

In ThoughtLeaders' sold-deal data, median CPMs ran from $36 for channels under 100K subscribers down to $17 for channels over 5M. 1of10 puts the typical range at $10 to $50. Where you land depends on niche and how steady your views are.

### Should I charge per video or per 1,000 views?

Most small creators quote a flat fee per video, but calculate it from views x CPM so it's easy to defend. Some deals use a flat base plus a bonus per signup or view milestone.

### How much should I charge for a dedicated video vs an integration?

A dedicated video is usually 2x to 4x a 60-90 second integration, according to 1of10. Aspire's survey found integrations at about 50-60% of the dedicated rate.

### Do subscribers matter at all?

They set the rough band, but views matter more inside it. Two channels with the same subscriber count can have very different views per video, and sponsors pay for views.
