# Newsletter sponsorship rates: how much to charge for a newsletter ad

A simple starting point is 2.5% to 5% of your subscriber count per ad, so about $250-$500 for a 10,000-person list. Across newsletters that publish a price, the median main sponsorship slot is $352 per issue, according to Book a Sponsor.

Below: what newsletters actually charge by list size and niche, real published rate cards, and how to price your secondary slots and dedicated sends.

## What newsletters charge, by list size

[Book a Sponsor read the printed prices of hundreds of newsletters](https://bookasponsor.com/newsletter-sponsorship-statistics) in November 2026. Their main findings:

- The median main slot is **$352**; the middle half ask **$200 to $800**.
- Across 406 newsletters that print both a price and a list size, the median works out to **$26 per 1,000 subscribers**.
- Lists **under 5,000 subscribers** ask a median **$175** a slot, about **$68 per 1,000** subscribers.
- Lists of **100,000+** ask a median **$1,200**, about **$7 per 1,000**.

So small lists charge far more per reader. That makes sense: a small, focused list is easier for a sponsor to target.

The beehiiv pricing guide, [summarized by Media List](https://medialist.com/article/129/newsletter-advertising-rates), gives wider bands:

| List size | Per placement |
|---|---|
| Under 5,000 | $50 - $250 |
| 5,000 - 50,000 | $500 - $3,000 (most land at $1,000 - $1,500) |
| 50,000+ | $3,000 - $20,000 |

## The 2.5%-5% rule

[Paved's pricing guide](https://www.paved.com/blog/newsletter-sponsorship-rates) suggests a flat fee of 2.5% to 5% of your subscriber count for your first ads:

- 3,000 subscribers: about $75 - $150 per placement
- 5,000 subscribers: about $125 - $250
- 10,000 subscribers: about $250 - $500

Go toward the top of the range if your readers are high earners, like executives or tech and finance professionals.

Once you have open-rate data, switch to CPM pricing: **(subscribers / 1,000) x your target CPM**. Paved's example: a marketing newsletter with 20,000 subscribers and a 60% open rate (well above the 40% category average) could justify a $35-$40 CPM, or about $700-$800 for the primary placement.

## Niche matters more than size

Some of the biggest lists earn the least per reader. Media List reported Q3 2026 marketplace benchmarks by category, per 1,000 subscribers:

| Category | Avg list size | Price per 1,000 subscribers |
|---|---|---|
| HR | 24,439 | $18.47 |
| Marketing | 57,780 | $13.88 |
| Tech | 166,912 | $4.76 |
| Lifestyle | 766,110 | $1.22 |

HR newsletters earned about 15x more per subscriber than lifestyle newsletters. If your list serves people who make buying decisions at work, price like it.

## Real published rate cards

These are actual prices newsletters list on their media kits, via [Media List](https://medialist.com/article/129/newsletter-advertising-rates):

- **OpenLampTech** (1,150+ readers): $55 for the top-of-email ad with logo; $20 for a mid-email shout-out; $7 for a classified.
- **Write the Docs** (12,000 subscribers): $500 per monthly issue.
- **Postgres Weekly** (17,501 subscribers): $1,280 primary slot, about $73 per 1,000.
- **Ruby Weekly** (35,565 subscribers): $1,220 primary slot, about $34 per 1,000.
- **JavaScript Weekly** (163,202 subscribers): $3,680 primary slot, about $23 per 1,000; a classified is $250.

Notice Postgres Weekly charges more per reader than JavaScript Weekly despite being a tenth the size. Narrow, technical B2B audiences are worth more to sponsors.

## How to price your other slots

Once your main slot sells, you can add more inventory. Two sources agree on rough ratios:

| Slot | Paved | Book a Sponsor (median) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary / main | 1x | 1x |
| Secondary (mid-content) | 0.50 - 0.65x | 0.60x |
| Classified (footer/link) | 0.25 - 0.35x | 0.40x |
| Dedicated send | 2 - 5x | 2.34x |

So if your main slot is $300, a secondary ad around $180, a classified around $100, and a dedicated email around $700 would all be in line.

## When to raise your rate

Paved lists the clearest signs you're underpriced:

- Sponsors book right away with no pushback on price.
- You're sold out weeks in advance.
- Sponsors keep rebooking.
- Your click-through rate beats your category average.

Their suggestion: revisit rates every six months or so, and raise 10-20% if demand and performance are strong.

## A quick pricing checklist

1. Start with 2.5%-5% of your subscriber count, or Book a Sponsor's size-band medians.
2. Adjust for niche: B2B, HR and tech-for-developers go higher; broad lifestyle goes lower.
3. Adjust for engagement: well-above-average open rates justify a higher CPM.
4. Price secondary, classified and dedicated slots off your main slot.
5. Write it all on a simple rate card and update it twice a year.

## FAQ

### How much should I charge for a newsletter ad with 1,000 subscribers?

The 2.5%-5% rule gives $25-$50. Published examples at that size exist: OpenLampTech, with 1,150+ readers, charges $55 for its top ad. Lists under 5,000 have a median of $175 per slot in Book a Sponsor's data, but that includes lists close to 5,000.

### What is a good CPM for a newsletter?

Book a Sponsor's overall median is $26 per 1,000 subscribers, with small lists around $68 and 100K+ lists around $7. Niche lists like Postgres Weekly charge about $73.

### Is newsletter CPM based on subscribers or opens?

Both exist. Many rate cards and benchmarks quote per 1,000 subscribers. Some sponsors prefer per 1,000 opens. Say which one you mean on your rate card.

### How much more is a dedicated send?

Book a Sponsor found a median of 2.34x the main slot. Paved suggests 2-5x.
