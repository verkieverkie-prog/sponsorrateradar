# Podcast sponsorship rates

Most podcast ads are priced per 1,000 downloads, and a host-read mid-roll typically goes for $15 to $40 CPM in 2026. Small shows under about 2,000 downloads an episode usually skip CPM and charge a flat $50 to $200 per episode instead.

Here's where those numbers come from, how ad slot and length change the price, and how to quote if your show is small.

## How podcast ads are priced

Podcast sponsorships almost always use CPM: cost per 1,000 downloads, usually counted within 30 days of an episode's release. The math:

**(Downloads per episode / 1,000) x CPM = price per ad**

So a show averaging 5,000 downloads per episode, selling a mid-roll at a $25 CPM, would charge $125 for that ad.

- **Mid-roll beats pre-roll beats post-roll**, and 60-second reads beat 30-second ones.

## Niche matters

Sponsorable notes that business, finance and B2B shows clear **$40+ CPMs** in multiple sources. Their example: a show reaching 5,000 CFOs is worth more per listener than a show reaching 500,000 commuters. If your audience buys expensive things at work, price toward the top of the range or above it.

## What small podcasts actually charge

CPM math breaks down for small shows. At 1,000 downloads and a $25-$40 CPM, a mid-roll would be $25-$40 per episode, which is barely worth a sponsor's time to set up.

So small shows usually price per episode. Sponsorable's guide lays it out by download count:

| Downloads per episode | CPM math ($25-$40) | What usually works |
|---|---|---|
| 500 - 2,000 | $12 - $80 per episode | Flat per-episode price ($50-$200 by niche), or affiliate/hybrid deals |
| 2,000 - 10,000 | $50 - $400 per episode | Flat per-episode rates based on CPM math; sell 3-4 episode bundles |
| 10,000 - 50,000 | $250 - $2,000 per episode | Quote CPM; discount for multi-month commitments |
| 50,000+ | $1,250+ per episode | Quote CPM; expect professional buyers and tracking requirements |

If you're under 2,000 downloads, a flat $50-$200 for a host-read mid-roll is the normal range, depending on niche. Affiliate deals, where the brand pays per sale through your code or link, are another common first step while you prove your show drives purchases.

## Price the read, not just the audience

Sponsorable makes a good point: a 90-second personal endorsement with a custom offer code is a different product from a 15-second script read. Price them differently and say so on your rate card.

A simple way to structure it:

1. **Base product:** 60-second host-read mid-roll at your CPM.
2. **Cheaper option:** 30-second read or pre-roll at a lower CPM (Libsyn lists $18-$20 for 30s vs $25 for 60s).
3. **Premium option:** longer personal endorsement, or a bundle across several episodes.

## How to negotiate

Three rules from Sponsorable's guide:

- **Name a range, not a number.** "Our mid-rolls run $28-$35 CPM depending on flight length" starts a conversation. A single number starts a discount.
- **Know your floor before the call.** Programmatic or affiliate income is your fallback, so a direct deal has to beat it.
- **Reward commitment, not single spots.** Give volume discounts for multi-episode bookings instead of cutting the price of one ad.

On payment, Sponsorable notes that direct deals commonly invoice on air date or net-30. For a first deal with a brand you don't know, half up front and half on air is a normal ask.

## Should you bother with programmatic?

Programmatic ads (inserted automatically by your host or an ad network) run about half the CPM of host-read ads, but they're passive. Sponsorable says many shows run programmatic as a floor and sell host-read ads directly on top. That's a reasonable setup for a growing show.

## Quick pricing steps

1. Find your average downloads per episode at 30 days.
2. If it's under about 2,000, set a flat per-episode price in the $50-$200 range based on your niche.
3. If it's above that, pick a CPM: start near $25 for a 60s host-read mid-roll, go higher for business or finance audiences.
4. Set lower prices for pre-roll, post-roll and 30-second reads.
5. Offer a 3-4 episode bundle.

## FAQ

### What is the average CPM for a podcast ad?

For a host-read mid-roll, published 2026 benchmarks mostly fall between $25 and $40. ADOPTER Media's data from 150,000+ placed ads shows a lower, wider $15-$30 range.

### How much can a podcast with 1,000 downloads charge?

CPM math gives about $25-$40 per episode, which is why small shows usually charge a flat $50-$200 per episode depending on niche, or use affiliate deals, per Sponsorable.

### Is pre-roll or mid-roll worth more?

Mid-roll. ADOPTER Media lists host-read mid-roll at $15-$30 CPM, pre-roll at $15-$25, and post-roll at $5-$10.

### Do podcast sponsors pay up front?

It varies. Direct deals often invoice on air date or net-30. For a first deal with a new brand, asking for half up front and half on air is normal.

### Are podcast ad rates negotiable?

Almost always. Published CPMs are a starting point. Episode bundles, ad length and exclusivity all move the final price.
