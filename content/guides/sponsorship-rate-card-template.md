# Sponsorship rate card template for creators

A sponsorship rate card is a one-page list of what you sell, what each thing costs, and the terms that come with it. Below is a free template you can copy, the eight fields brands expect to see, and real sourced prices to help you fill in the numbers.

## What a rate card needs

[Influencer Advisory](https://influenceradvisory.com/blog/creator-rate-card-template/) lists eight fields brand buyers look for. Most creator rate cards skip a few, which leads to a week of back-and-forth emails.

1. **Deliverable.** Be specific: "1 integrated YouTube segment," not "1 video."
2. **Format and length.** Dedicated, integrated or mention; length in seconds or minutes.
3. **Exclusivity window.** How long you won't work with a competitor. 30 days is the usual default.
4. **Usage rights.** Whether the brand can rerun your content as an ad. Price it as its own line.
5. **Revisions.** Two rounds is the common default, with a fee for extras.
6. **Payment terms.** For example 50% on signing and 50% on publish, or net-15/net-30.
7. **Cancellation policy.** Influencer Advisory's suggested default: 50% if cancelled after the brief is approved, 100% if cancelled after a draft is delivered.
8. **Base fee.** The price.

## Copy-paste template (YouTube)

Replace the bracketed parts with your own numbers.

```
[CHANNEL NAME] - SPONSORSHIP RATES ([MONTH YEAR])

Audience
- Median views per video (last 10, at 30 days): [X]
- Top countries: [X]
- Main age range: [X]
- Topic: [one line]

Placements
- 60-90s integrated segment ............ $[BASE]
- 30s mention ........................... $[0.5-0.7 x BASE]
- Dedicated video ....................... $[2-4 x BASE]
- Sponsored Short ....................... $[0.25-0.5 x BASE]
- Bundle: 1 integration + 3 Shorts ....... $[1.5-1.8 x BASE]

Add-ons
- Description link + pinned comment ..... +5-10%
- Exclusivity (30 days, category) ....... +20-50%
- Paid ad usage rights .................. +30-100%
- Extra revision (after 2 included) ..... $[X] each

Terms
- Payment: 50% on signing, 50% on publish
- Cancellation: 50% after brief approval, 100% after draft delivered
- All sponsored content is disclosed per FTC and YouTube rules

Contact: [EMAIL]
```

The multipliers come from [1of10's 2026 rate guide](https://www.1of10.com/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rates/) (format multipliers, usage rights and exclusivity add-ons) and [Dangaro](https://dangaro.com/en/blog/youtube-shorts-sponsorship-cost) (long-form + 3 Shorts at 150-180% of the long-form rate).

## How to set your base fee

Your base fee is your standard 60-90 second integration. Work it out from views:

**(Median views per video / 1,000) x CPM = base fee**

For the CPM, [ThoughtLeaders' data on 9,002 sold integrations](https://thoughtleaders.ai/blog/2026-10-15-youtube-sponsorship-cost-2026/) gives these medians:

| Channel size | Median price | Median CPM |
|---|---|---|
| Under 100K subs | $600 | $36 |
| 100K - 500K subs | $1,200 | $26 |
| 500K - 1M subs | $2,000 | $21 |
| 1M - 5M subs | $3,500 | $19 |

Influencer Advisory's own data on 766 YouTube creators gives a median fee of $1,000 under 50K subs, $1,715 at 50K-250K, $4,500 at 250K-1M, and $7,500 at 1M+.

Not sure which CPM to use? The [sponsorship rate calculator](/sponsorrateradar/calculator.html) suggests one by niche, and the [rates pages](/sponsorrateradar/rates/) show every sourced data point for your niche.

## A filled-in example

Say you run an education channel with a median of 20,000 views at 30 days, mostly US viewers.

- CPM: $32 (between the sub-100K median of $36 and the middle of [TrendReport's](https://trendreport.com/intelligence/youtube-sponsorship-rates-by-niche/) $16-$42 education range)
- Base fee: 20 x $32 = **$640**

Then:

- 30s mention: $320 - $450
- Dedicated video: $1,280 - $2,560
- Sponsored Short: $160 - $320
- Integration + 3 Shorts: $960 - $1,150
- 30-day exclusivity: +$128 - $320
- Paid usage rights: +$192 - $640

Round these to clean numbers. Brands don't need to see your math, but you should be able to explain it if asked.

## Newsletter rate card template

Newsletters use slots instead of video formats. Price everything off your main slot:

```
[NEWSLETTER NAME] - SPONSORSHIP RATES ([MONTH YEAR])

Audience
- Subscribers: [X]   - Avg open rate: [X]%   - Avg click rate: [X]%
- Who reads it: [one line]

Placements (per issue)
- Primary slot (top) ...................... $[MAIN]
- Secondary slot (mid) .................... $[~0.6 x MAIN]
- Classified (text + link) ................ $[~0.25-0.4 x MAIN]
- Dedicated send .......................... $[~2-5 x MAIN]

Packages
- 4 issues, primary slot .................. $[X]

Terms: [payment terms], [booking lead time]
Contact: [EMAIL]
```

The ratios come from [Paved](https://www.paved.com/blog/newsletter-sponsorship-rates) (secondary 50-65%, classifieds 25-35%, dedicated 2-5x) and [Book a Sponsor](https://bookasponsor.com/newsletter-sponsorship-statistics) (medians of 0.60x, 0.40x and 2.34x). For a first main-slot price, Paved suggests 2.5%-5% of your subscriber count, so $250-$500 at 10,000 subscribers.

## Podcast rate card template

Podcasts price per 1,000 downloads. List your average downloads per episode at 30 days, then:

- 60s host-read mid-roll at your CPM
- 30s read or pre-roll at a lower CPM
- A 3-4 episode bundle

For reference, [Sponsorable's roundup](https://sponsorable.com/guides/podcast-ad-rates) lists host-read mid-roll at $15-$30 CPM (ADOPTER Media's placed-ads data) and $25 for a 60s read on Libsyn Ads ($18-$20 for 30s). Small shows usually charge a flat $50-$200 per episode instead.

## Should you publish your rate card?

You don't have to. Many creators keep it private and send it when a brand asks. Influencer Advisory says that for channels under 250K subscribers, a public rate card usually doesn't hurt your negotiating power.

Either way, put a date on it and update it at least twice a year. [Paved suggests](https://www.paved.com/blog/newsletter-sponsorship-rates) reviewing rates about every six months and raising them 10-20% if demand and performance are strong.

## FAQ

### What should be on a sponsorship rate card?

The eight fields buyers expect: deliverable, format and length, exclusivity, usage rights, revisions, payment terms, cancellation policy and base fee.

### Should I put CPM on my rate card?

It's optional. Influencer Advisory says many creators list it as a derived number (base fee divided by typical views, times 1,000) to help brands compare.

### What if a brand offers free product instead of money?

Influencer Advisory suggests keeping the fee line and writing "$0 base fee, X dollars retail value of product" so the trade is clear.

### How often should I update my rate card?

At least every six months, or whenever your median views change a lot. Date every version.
