# YouTube Shorts sponsorship rates

A sponsored YouTube Short usually sells for about 30% to 50% of the same creator's long-form rate. In Dangaro's 2026 analysis of 38,000+ channels, that works out to roughly $150 to $1,000 per Short at 10K-50K subscribers and $500 to $2,000 at 50K-100K.

Below are the ranges by channel size, why Shorts price lower even though they often get more views, and how to package them so they're worth your time.

## Shorts rates by channel size

[Dangaro's Shorts pricing guide](https://dangaro.com/en/blog/youtube-shorts-sponsorship-cost) (July 2026, US/English market) gives these per-Short ranges, next to long-form rates for the same channel size:

| Subscribers | Long-form rate | Shorts rate |
|---|---|---|
| 10K - 50K | $500 - $3,000 | $150 - $1,000 |
| 50K - 100K | $1,500 - $5,000 | $500 - $2,000 |
| 100K - 300K | $3,000 - $8,000 | $1,000 - $3,000 |
| 300K - 500K | $5,000 - $12,000 | $1,800 - $4,500 |
| 500K - 1M | $8,000 - $20,000 | $2,500 - $7,500 |
| 1M+ | $15,000 - $50,000+ | $5,000 - $18,000+ |

Other sources land in a similar place:

- [CollabPals' rate calculator](https://www.collabpals.com/tools/youtube-sponsorship-rate-calculator) puts a sponsored Short at **$200 to $1,500** for a 100,000-subscriber channel, often sold in multi-Short packages.
- [1of10](https://www.1of10.com/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rates/) prices a sponsored Short at **0.25x to 0.5x** a standard 60-90 second integration.

## Why Shorts cost less (even with more views)

Dangaro found Shorts often reach 100-300% of a channel's subscriber count in views, versus 30-50% for long-form. So why do they sell for less?

- **Attention is shallower.** People swipe fast. A viewer who watches a 10-minute sponsored review is far more engaged than one who scrolls past a 30-second ad.
- **They don't last.** Dangaro puts a Short's lifespan at days to weeks, versus months to years for long-form.
- **Less production.** A Short is usually quicker to make, so there's less work to charge for.

That means Shorts are usually bought for reach and awareness, not for in-depth product explanations. Dangaro says they're a weak fit for complex products, B2B services and expensive, high-consideration purchases.

## Price Shorts off Shorts views, not channel views

If your Shorts and your long-form videos get very different views, price each from its own numbers. Dangaro's advice to brands is to judge a Shorts creator on average Shorts views, not subscriber count. Their example: a creator with 50K subscribers and 500K average Shorts views is worth far more for a Shorts campaign than one with 500K subscribers and 20K average Shorts views.

A simple method:

1. Find the median views of your last 10-20 Shorts.
2. Work out your long-form integration rate (views x CPM; the [calculator](/sponsorrateradar/calculator.html) does this for you).
3. Price a sponsored Short at 30-50% of that, then check it against the size table above.
4. If your Shorts views are consistently huge compared with your long-form, lean toward the top of the range.

### A note on view counts

On August 24, 2026, YouTube changed how it counts public long-form views. [ThoughtLeaders reports](https://thoughtleaders.ai/blog/2026-10-15-youtube-sponsorship-cost-2026/) that Shorts view counts did not change, so older Shorts benchmarks still compare like-for-like with your current numbers.

## Bundles are where Shorts make sense

Selling one Short at a time is a lot of back-and-forth for a small fee. Packages work better for both sides. From Dangaro's guide:

- **Long-form + Shorts bundle:** one long-form integration plus 3 Shorts typically costs **150-180% of the long-form rate alone**.
- **Shorts series:** booking 3-5 Shorts released over 1-2 weeks reduces the per-unit cost by **15-25%** for the brand.
- Bundles pairing long-form with Shorts now make up **about half** of all sponsorship deals, per Dangaro.

So if your long-form integration is $800, a bundle of that plus 3 Shorts at $1,200-$1,440 is in line with the market. Each Short is effectively $133-$213, but you get one deal, one brief and one invoice.

## Rates are rising

Dangaro says Shorts sponsorship rates are up **20-30% year over year in 2026**, the biggest jump of any YouTube format, while overall sponsorship rates rose 10-15% versus 2025. If you set your Shorts rate last year, it's probably time to check it.

## What to include in a Shorts deal

- **Length and placement.** Is the sponsor the whole Short or a mention?
- **Number of Shorts and timing.** A series over 1-2 weeks is common.
- **Usage rights.** If the brand wants to run your Short as a paid ad, that's an extra fee. 1of10 lists +30 to +100 percent of the base fee for usage rights.
- **Exclusivity.** Not working with competitors for 30-90 days is also extra; 1of10 puts it at +20 to +50 percent.
- **Market.** Dangaro notes emerging markets typically see 30-60% lower pricing, so mention where your audience is if it's mostly US/UK/Canada/Australia.

## FAQ

### How much should I charge for a sponsored YouTube Short?

Start at 30-50% of your long-form integration rate. Dangaro's ranges are $150-$1,000 at 10K-50K subs and $500-$2,000 at 50K-100K subs.

### Are Shorts sponsorships worth it for small creators?

They can be, especially in a bundle. One long-form integration plus 3 Shorts typically sells for 150-180% of the long-form rate, which is more total money for one deal.

### Should I price Shorts by subscribers or views?

By your Shorts views. Shorts and long-form audiences can be very different sizes, and sponsors increasingly look at Shorts-specific numbers.

### Did YouTube's 2026 view-count change affect Shorts?

No. ThoughtLeaders reports that the August 24, 2026 change affected long-form public view counts; Shorts counts didn't change.
